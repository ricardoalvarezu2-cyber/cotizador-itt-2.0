Concentric and Eccentric Reducer Dimensions

A reducer changes pipe size in a single butt-welded fitting. Concentric reducers keep both ends on a common centerline; eccentric reducers share a flat side instead, which keeps either the top or the bottom of the line running straight through the size change.

Concentric or eccentric is not a style choice.

On a horizontal line, a concentric reducer creates a high point where vapour collects and a low point where liquid and solids settle. That is why pump suction lines use an eccentric reducer flat side up. It keeps the top of the pipe level so air cannot pocket ahead of the pump and cause cavitation. On vertical lines the distinction stops mattering and concentric is normal.

Flat side up or flat side down.

Flat side up on pump suction, to prevent vapour pockets. Flat side down where you want the line to drain completely and keep solids moving. Getting this backwards is one of the most common and most expensive piping mistakes in rotating equipment work.

Reach for it when

  • ›Concentric: vertical runs and any line where trapped vapour is not a concern.
  • ›Eccentric flat side up: horizontal pump suction, to keep air out of the pump.
  • ›Eccentric flat side down: lines that must drain fully or carry settling solids.

How To Read These Dimensions

ASME B16.9 covers concentric and eccentric reducers, and gives both the same overall length L at a given size pairing. The standard sets the dimensions; it does not tell you which of the two the service needs, and that is the part worth getting right.

O.D.
Outside diameterOutside diameter at the welding end, matching the pipe it welds to.
I.D.
Inside diameterBore through the fitting, set by the wall thickness.
T
Wall thicknessNominal wall at the welding end, matched to the pipe schedule.
L
Overall lengthFace to face, end to end. Concentric and eccentric reducers share the same L at a given size pairing.

Common Questions

What is the difference between a concentric and an eccentric reducer?+

A concentric reducer keeps both ends on the same centerline, so the size change is symmetrical. An eccentric reducer offsets the centerlines so one side of the fitting stays flat and in line with the pipe. On horizontal runs that flat side is what stops vapour or liquid being trapped at the size change.

Why is an eccentric reducer used on pump suction?+

Installed flat side up, it keeps the top of the suction line level through the size change so air cannot collect in a high pocket ahead of the pump. Trapped vapour entering a pump causes cavitation, loss of head, and impeller damage.

Which way up does an eccentric reducer go?+

Flat side up on pump suction, to prevent vapour pockets. Flat side down where the priority is draining the line or keeping settling solids moving. The service dictates it, and the fitting must be installed accordingly.

How are reducers sized?+

Large end × small end. A 6 × 4 reducer takes 6" pipe down to 4". The tables above give outside diameter, inside diameter, and wall thickness for both ends, plus the overall length L.
