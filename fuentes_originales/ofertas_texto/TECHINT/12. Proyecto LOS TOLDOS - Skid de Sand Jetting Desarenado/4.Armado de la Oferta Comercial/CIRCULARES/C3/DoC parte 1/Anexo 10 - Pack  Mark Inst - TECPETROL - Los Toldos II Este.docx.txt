Packing & Marking Instructions
Purpose
This document is intended to give packaging and marking instructions. The Supplier will have to comply with the norms included in the present Document, to which the goods for transport must respond. The Supplier shall be responsible for packaging goods in seaworthy packing (or for air/truck transportation if it is the case) to withstand the long distance ocean and inland transportation and numerous handling for loading and unloading. All possible protection measures shall be taken by Supplier to prevent damage from moisture, rain, rust, shock and corrosion according to the different characteristics of goods. The cost of loss and/or damage due to inadequate packaging shall be Supplier’s sole liability and be at his expense.

Packing Specifications
General
Prerequisites for Packaging
The goods shall not be packed until Purchaser’s release report has been granted. The requirements for the protection, preparation and identification of the goods are part of the manufacturing specification and are therefore not included in the scope of packaging work.
Protective Measures 
If any protection such as grease, wax, oil or paint has been applied, the goods shall not be packed until the coating goods have cured and no longer produce gaseous emissions.
Preparation for Packaging
Before packing the goods it must be ensured that moving parts or parts which may be damaged by mechanical stresses during transportation have been appropriately fixed and braced. All openings have to be capped.
This instruction provides the minimum suggested requirements for the preservation, packaging and packing of equipment for international overseas shipment, unless otherwise specified in the Purchase Order. It is of Vendor's exclusive responsibility to select and use the means to guarantee the compliance of the requirements in this document. Workmanship shall be of the highest standards throughout all operations. Material used shall be in accordance with the best commercial export practices. It shall be capable of performing all its functions without damage to the commodity content. Methods used shall be such as to warrant safe delivery of the commodity to the consignment destination. Skid mounted equipment must be shipped with lifting lugs installed to allow handling with overhand cranes, lifting points and centers of gravity must be clearly marked. Containers will be packed in such a manner to insure an even distribution of weight within the container. All cases will bear warning signs on the outside denoting the center of gravity and sling marks. Heavy machinery which includes tables, rollers, counterweights, machine heads or other movable parts will be blocked and braced to prevent movement. Accessories which cannot be blocked or braced will be removed and packed as a separate item within the main container. Top heavy cases and containers will be so marked as either "Top Heavy" or "Heavy End". International symbols in addition to English wording are required for all markings
All shipments must be prepared and packed to provide for adequate protection if shipped as "on deck" cargo followed by outdoor storage of twelve (12) months at the Jobsite

The packaged goods have to be marked in conformance with the Shipping Procedure for Supplier.

The goods will be transported by different transports: Aerial, Marine, Train, Terrestrial, etc
The goods will frequently be handled or transferred by different forms and with different loading equipment (crane, clams, etc.) or even manually
The goods can be kept under cover (permanently or temporarily)

Packaging
General
The packing will be made fully complying to the following specifications: 
The inner and outer packages shall conform to HPE standard requirements (latest version). Deviations are permissible only if approved by the Purchaser in writing. Deviations from HPE standard required by Purchaser have been considered in this standard. Seaworthy cases/crates shall be of special type B2 as described in the HPE standard.
Package Requirements
All packages such as cases, crates, timber and other structures shall ensure that the packaged goods are effectively protected against partial or complete loss and damage.
The packages shall be carefully designed for the climatic, static and dynamic loads occurring during transportation, transshipment and storage, e.g.
Outdoor and hold atmosphere
Longitudinal, bending, shear and transverse compression stresses
Surface pressures
Stacking pressures
Jolts during marshalling
Vibrations
Cost-effectiveness shall be appropriately considered.
The sizes and types of packages shall be suitable for the properties and design of the packaged goods. To minimize the risk of losses, small packages measuring less than 0.35 m3 and /or weighing less than 150 kg should be avoided. In such cases, the Purchaser’s shipping department shall be informed early enough before packaging begins to ensure that suitable part-load combinations can be identified.
The packages shall be designed with a view to:
Making optimum use of available vehicle space
Avoiding oversized
Avoiding waste of space in the packages
Efficient transshipment and storage methods
Effective and quick unpacking of the goods at their final locations
The packages for goods which are classified as hazardous substances within the meaning of the law and therefore require appropriate identification according to the hazchem list shall in all cases conform to the respective carriers’ regulations.
It is mandatory to put identifying label (for stroke, inclination, humidity, etc) on all the package depending on the merchandise nature.

Bundles  
Structural Components  
Bundles are transport units where the packing measurements are restricted to the combination between several parts for a safe transport, doing that they are stackable and creating lifting possibilities for industrial cranes and trucks.
Consequently, only those goods that absorb the loading pressures and cannot bend can be tied. 
Bundles with cables, steel or textile tape must be avoided because they are subject to deformations. 
The custom is to use screwed wood closings or, for greater net weights, steel fixations with wood inner linings. 
The closings must be closed with force to prevent that the packed pieces slide towards the outside of the bundle even though it is not loaded flat.  This must be assured after several maneuvers and prolonged storage. 
A bundle must be tied with 3 moorings. The distance between moorings must not exceed 2.50m.  The square beams to be used must be adequate for the stress being applied.  The lateral projection of the mooring beams must not be less than 10cm.  Under no circumstance can they be under 12mm. 
Stuffing tapes must be nailed to the beams, so that the heads do not protrude. 
The nuts and screws of the beams must have washers 436 DIN or DIN 440.  The nuts must be well tight to prevent them from coming loose.  Screws that protrude must be shortened to the necessary measurements after tightening. 
Any necessary intermediate layer must be made of 100 AW plated wood.  Marking surfaces or terminals must be attached to the beams. 
The intermediate layers can be solid wood beams. They must have the length of the brackets of the turnbuckles.  The ends are holed and placed on the threaded beams of the turnbuckles.  This prevents them from sliding or falling.  Short beams or blocks are not allowed as intermediate layers. 
Vertical beams in the threaded rods must be avoided or cut shorter than these, so that the turnbuckles can be re-tensed. 
Terminal points exposed to the risk of being twisted, and any terminals that have been specially machined, must be protected against impact by covers. 
The superior and inferior brackets of the turnbuckles cannot be made of simple angular sections. These can bend.  U sections, I or beams must be used for this intention.  The maximum distance between turnbuckles is 3m, and 1m towards the ends of the bundle.  The distance between the threaded rods of a turnbuckle is 1.20 m. 
Singular beams must not have attached turnbuckles but instead mooring rings.  The sling points must be most as far away from the center of gravity as is possible, and marked. 
Bundles that contain pieces of different lengths must be designed of such a form that the longest pieces are at the bottom and the shortest above.  Such a bundle must have a pyramid form.  Individual pieces that protrude extremely must be bundled separately. 
Bundles with Filter Plates  
Bulks with filter plates must be designed with beams of square wood support next to the turnbuckles of the bundles so that they can be piled up. 
Stringers must avoid the risk of damage by the normal use of forklifts.  The crossed pieces for these bulks must have hooks with short chains.
Precipitation Grills  
Precipitation grills for the flue-gas filter must be packed in crates. Bundles are not allowed. 
Anticorrosive agents that form a film must be applied to cover the surface of the filter plates and grills taking in consideration the climatic conditions.
Bundles of Boiler Tubes
The steel tape must have a crossed section of at least 30 x 1 mm and a breakage strength of 2,4 ton.  In the straight section (The sealing piece reduces the breakage point to 80%=1.9 ton). 
If the tubes are provided in fixed lengths, the ends must be protected against impact and damage. 
This can be obtained by means of molds for the ends or similar measure like a flexible neck and wood cover. 
Practice for Transportation of Pipes on Barges and Marine Vessels
Vessel Condition
Cargo compartments should be reasonably free from any foreign objects or material likely to cause either physical damage, contamination, or chemical reaction with the pipe.
The bilge pumping system shall be in working order to remove standing water from the cargo hold.
Nominated vessel must be a box shape type in order to facilitate the loading/ unloading process and guarantee the Security of the cargo
Shipping Space
Pipe may be laid longitudinally or athwart the vessel to make the best use of available space. A clearance of 1 foot must be left between the ends of the pipe and the vessel (or other cargo) facilitate unloading. Dimensions of hatchways should be large enough to allow the pipe to pass in a horizontal position unless special precautions are taken.
Handling Equipment
When end hooks are used for handling pipe, they shall be designed to prevent end damage and should be lined in the area of land (bevel face) contact with a cushioning material (for example, a nonmetallic material such as rubber or a metallic material such as aluminum, but brass or copper shall be excluded). These hooks shall also have sufficient width and depth to fit the internal curvature of the pipe. Rubber aprons should be attached to pipe hooks to protect the pipe ends unless adequate end protectors are used. Lifting shall be carried out in such a manner that impact loads sufficient to cause local denting or out-of-roundness of pipe body or pipe ends will not occur. When the pipe is loaded by loose lifts, all necessary precautions shall be taken during loading and unloading to prevent surface or other damage to the pipe.
Staking Arrangement
Stowage
When stacking, one should consider the maximum weight that the bottom layer of pipe can withstand before deformation will occur. Short lengths should be placed on the top of the stack.
Pipe in the hold of a vessel during marine shipments shall be cantline stowed.
Cantline stowage shall be defined as stowage without separator strips (for example, nesting or pyramid fashion) but including wood blocking every other tier on both sides of the hull as illustrated in Figure 1. If the top tier is a partial load, separator strips shall be used to secure additional blocking, which is used to secure the top tier of pipe.
For inland waterway transit, pipe may be cantline stowed, or separator strips may be used between successive tiers of pipe.
Loaded pipe shall not contact the sides or bottom of the vessel. However, each length of pipe shall be in contact throughout its entire length with all adjacent pipe or blocking, and precautions should be taken to minimize any lateral movement.
Pipe with filler metal weld seams (SAW and GMAW) shall be positioned or padded in such a manner that the weld does not contact the blocking or adjacent pip. When horizontal stripping (permissible only for transportation on inland waterways ) is used to load straight filler metal weld seam pipe (SAW and GMAW), THE WELDSEAM SHALL BE POSITIONED AT 45 DEGREES, +5 degrees, from vertical. When nested, pipe with a straight filler metal weld seam (SAW and GMAW) shall be positioned with the seam at 0 degrees (in other words, 12 o’clock).
Stacking limitation shall be take in account when the stowing plan is arranged between ocean carrier and supplier.
Bearing and Separator Strips
Wood-bearing and separator strips shall be a minimum size of 1 inch x 2 inches. Metallic-bearing strips are prohibited.
Bearing strips shall be used to keep the bottom layer of pipe above the hold bottom. The spacing of these strips should be as small as necessary but no greater than 4 feet unless stress concentration calculations from Section 3 allow wider spacing to be used. For any loading arrangement, however, at least 4 bearing strips per pipe stack must be used. For inland waterway shipments, horizontal separator strips may be used when the pipe is not nested. These horizontal strips should be located directly above the bottom bearing strips.
Additional blocking, if necessary, shall be used to minimize lateral movement of pipe.
Side Protection
To prevent stress concentrations, wooden side-bearing strips or wood blocking shall be provided so that contact with the hull of the vessel or any protrusion is prevented.
Inspection
Purchaser/Insurance/Final Client’s inspector shall have access to loading and unloading facilities. Please note that in condition of delivery: CFR, CIF, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU & DDP, the notice of loading/unloading shall be send with reasonable advance, no less than 15 running days before.
Loading
Damaged pipe shall not be loaded on board. If damaged pipe is detected on board, it should be noted on the bill of lading and the pipe marked by the carrier to indicate pre-transit damage.
Unloading
Pipe damage detected during transit or unloading should be promptly reported to the carrier and/or manufacturer and appropriately marked and set aside for further inspection.
Maritime Route
In order to minimize the possibility of damage, the maritime route to be used for the pipes shall be the North, through the Canal de Panamá.
